Gasoline Affordability and Unemployment

Last updated

November 27, 2026

Do weaker labor markets make gasoline less affordable?

Rising gasoline prices hurt most when household budgets are already strained. To see whether states with weaker labor markets also faced the steepest affordability burden, we compare each state’s unemployment rate with the number of hours of work needed to buy 15 gallons of regular gasoline in September 2026.

Hours per fill-up combines real gasoline prices, real weekly earnings, and average weekly hours:

\[ \text{Hours per fill-up} = \frac{15 \times \text{real gas price}}{\text{real weekly earnings} / \text{weekly hours}} \]

A higher value means gasoline is less affordable for a typical worker in that state.

Hover over any point to see the state and its values.

Regression results

Unemployment alone

The estimated slope is 0.01 hours per percentage point of unemployment (p = 0.76), and the correlation between the two variables is 0.05. Unemployment explains only 0.2% of the variation in hours per fill-up across states. The 95% confidence interval for the slope, from -0.054 to 0.073, includes zero.

Adding region

After accounting for region, the model explains 30.2% of the variation in hours per fill-up (adjusted R² = 0.24). Holding the unemployment rate constant, Western states required an estimated 0.18 more hours of work per fill-up than Midwestern states (p = 0.0047). The Northeast and South do not differ significantly from the Midwest.

Limitations

  • One month only. The analysis uses September 2026 so that each state is an independent observation. The relationship may differ in other months.
  • Correlation, not causation. The regression describes an association across states and cannot show that unemployment or region causes differences in affordability.
  • Average workers. Hours per fill-up uses each state’s average weekly earnings and hours. It does not reflect affordability for unemployed people or low-wage workers, who may be hit hardest.
  • Small sample. With 50 states, the model has limited power to detect weak relationships.
